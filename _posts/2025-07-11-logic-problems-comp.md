Problem 1- The Grand Master vowed to set up a truly fair test to reveal the best logician amongst his three friends (without giving an advantage to any one of them). He showed the three men 5 hats: two white and three black. Then he turned off the lights in the room and put a hat on each logician' head. The logicians would need to guess the color of their own hat. After that, the old sage hid the remaining two hats, but before he could turn the lights on, the logicians announced the correct colors of their hats. What was their reasoning?
-----
Solution- All three logicians had black hats on their head. The most important statement in this problem was that the Grand Master vowed to set up a truly fair test. This means that no one person should have an advantage when the lights are turned on. Looking at the possible cases of hat distributions, we can see that the cases are either WWB, WBB, or BBB. Looking at the first case we can see that the person with a black hat would know they had a black hat while the people with white hats wouldn't. This is an unfair advantage. Looking at the second case we can see that a person with a black hat would know they have a black hat because if they had a white hat then it would give an unfair advantage to the other person with a black hat. The person with a white hat in this case wouldn't know whether they had a black or white hat because they see two black hats which makes this case unfair as well. This means that the they all had black hats and they all could deduce this themselves.
